Why the kidney decides the blood pressure

Cardiac output sets the pressure minute to minute, but the kidney sets the volume the heart has to work with. Raise sodium intake and the extracellular volume expands; stretch the atria and ANP rises to spill the excess, while a falling pressure at the juxtaglomerular apparatus wakes RAAS — angiotensin II squeezing vessels within seconds, aldosterone rebuilding volume at the tubule over hours.

In the plate above, that tug-of-war is drawn, not just numbered: arterial calibre narrows as angiotensin II rises, the cortical wash deepens with filtration, and urine outflow quickens. Move Vascular tone and watch the vessels before you read the MAP tile — every control has a visible correlate.
