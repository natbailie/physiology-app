# Reference images

Drop images in here. This is the highest-signal thing you can give me about how the
diagrams should look, and it takes about twenty minutes.

## Naming

```
like-<source>-<subject>.png      a diagram you want ours to feel like
avoid-<source>-<subject>.png     a diagram you want ours to stay away from
```

Five to ten `like-`, two or three `avoid-`. The negatives matter more than they look:
they're what stops me reading a preference too widely.

## What to collect

Anything. Textbook plates, Osmosis or Ninja Nerd frames, Complete Anatomy, a
competitor app, a figure from a paper, a screenshot of something that isn't
physiology at all. It does not need to be a diagram of the thing the module covers —
if a subway map has the label discipline you want, that's a useful reference.

What I read out of an image that prose can't carry:

- line weight, and whether structures are outlined or massed
- label density per frame, and how labels attach — beside, leader, or on the shape
- how many colours appear at once, and what colour is being asked to mean
- how much white space, and how tightly the subject is framed
- how much anatomical detail is enough before it becomes noise

## Copyright

References **inform, they are never traced or reproduced.** Every diagram stays
independently drawn from the schema. A traced textbook figure is a commercial
liability in a paid product, so this is a hard line, not a preference.

Keep the files out of git if any are from a paid source — `docs/diagrams/references/`
is gitignored for exactly that reason.
